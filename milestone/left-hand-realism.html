Ahead: a fork and a stranger, approaching. Which way came the stranger? If in from the left, they took a right; if in from the right, a left.

Behold—

In from the right to the right? Take the left. The left the left the right.

Whosoever arrives off the left hand congeals to colour, to a fork-starer’s field of perception, at the walker-in’s most normative hour. Dusty and sinistral residued. With talk of my favourite Le Guin. It’s The Dispossessed, I say, or The Word for World is Forest or it’s all the Earthsea books so hard to choose. I lie. But I come toward the mortal and real. Asking for help. For medicine. As a beggar.

This ain't no dead piece of metal. A car's a living, breathing thing, and she's alive. Feel it talking to you. Telling you what she wants, what she needs. All you gotta do is listen. Close your eyes and listen. ⸺ The Wachowskis, Speed Racer

Let a machine - in the vulgar sense: metal, romex, battery acid – be set adrift, preserved, freed of inertia about twenty billion years, before it’s noticed by some unthinkable civilisation. Twenty billion's the number of years a star can support a planetary ecosystem before collapse, meaning – as an aggregate – when comparing mechanical and organic life, our universe's default mode of autopoiesis (life) is mechanical and i.e. technology is older (bigger on the timescale) than nature. In the vulgar sense. Mosquitoes, rot, nerves. And so doesn’t nature, really, endure at technology’s behest, not the reverse?

As a child, the above thought troubled me deeply, in fact lent itself as material for nightmares. Reflecting on that troubledness, now, as an adult, more than anything it reads as yet another – glaring, blatant – sign I was trans. Beginning by repeating, I need to share an Anti-Oedipus quote I once saw among the TV-sprayer’s lights and colours all over the bars of my and Cassandra’s cage during the centuries I whispered after and awaited her resurrection (Lyre 2020, “President Oedipus”). Discussed amid the colours and shadows thereout were the intersecting vitalities of capital and labour or machinery and humanity, as emblemised in clovers and bumble bees. Deleuze & Guattari: “The bumble bee is a part of the reproductive system of the clover… then why not we part of that of the machines?” In general, groups like mine and people like me share an appreciation of what it’s like for one’s young to be carried by a hegemonic other. We are born again and again from the bodies of our oppressors into the bastions of our enemies – and, as they are helpless to reproduce us, we are helpless to be reproduced. Home is elsewhere and nature, if nature is what they say it is, is not a friend. Intuitively, we sense we are larger than it, that we share some flicker of animist essence with the cheap, thrumming wires in the walls. Right from the outset, this manner of subject appreciates the dishonesty of their own eyes. They worry that to give any witness is to lie, and so – starting typing – the second they write “I” they are imperilled: should I lie and write so-called realism or tell the truth and write so-called speculative fiction?
