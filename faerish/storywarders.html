Clause & phrase syntax

  • Become-copula: ghiæan — Duílúch Fæ'du'ærs fæ ghiæan.
  • Order: O-S-V, except first person: S-O-V.
  • Dummy subjects: dummy-donors extend — Chair í gæn / Mith Me'hair'os gæn ("Rain it gives / We Rain-ours give").
  • Verbs freely objectless: Theváre fæ ("Fæ devours") — V+S intransitive when not 1sg/pl.
  • Indirect objects: a separate category — Duîm-fær niïl Rîbha fæ gæn ("To-with-fær a book fæ gave").
  • Other verbal arguments: tmetic, plus by, mé, duîm — duîm fith "to eat with", mé ios-thurn "with there", by ghiæan "get over".
  • Attested argument combinations: V+S; S+V (1sg/pl); OSV; SOV; IO+DO+S+V; S+IO+DO+V (1sg/pl); PP+S+V; S+PP+V (1sg/pl).
  • Constituent order: Isc mæ fith for all sentence-types except 1sg/pl; Oob lus Math also fine.
  • Adverbials tend to front. Types: time, place, manner; order can jumble for emphasis.
  • Obligatory adverbials: only the tmesis-nominated du, which needs a du-modified adverb for its being-given.
  • Adjective phrase, operational definition: wrappable in or…il; may host a noun; fits "aoa sin't __" ("seems/so-abouts __").
  • Adjective arguments: by the P stock or by tmesis; one argument per adjective; order PP–adjective (fær uperya e "her proud of").
  • Subjectless adjectives: Clach í bhie ("Cold it is"); Clach tens bhie ("Cold me is" — 1sg/pl without subject).
  • Adjectives take no direct objects — tmeses and compounds instead: Ten férás-ufsae más ("I boy-crazy am").
  • Adjective indirect objects via by-phrases: tes Sáinsu gáns by "my task-set useless via"; lus Sáinsu eës Hrádai fiïl by "the task-set of-the crown loyal via".
  • Other adjective arguments: e-phrases of content (Wíc yound e "of water full") and the double-negatived 'abouts' (aoa Frœ'thte eës "of the nearabouts / in the area").
  • Adjective-modifiers: adverbs (sinya holdh), set-nouns (mhís e Ho'du'ldh), PPs (holdh lus Sáinsu eës Lethr by), clauses, curses (holdh • O • Mee S'i'pún'il'n í aoa gæn), circumfixes — "muchly tall; lots of tall-towardness; tall for a halfling's purposes; tall—and woe if it gives no likeness-to-a-tower".
  • Degree on adjectives: circumfixal (Or…il / Ro…il) and clitic ('ya); putting those same markers directly on adjectives yields irregular emphasis.
  • Case-marked NP as adjective-modifier: Holdhmÿa iy tens "taller than me" (mÿa: positively contrary).
  • Nonfinite adverbial clause on an adjective: Or'threïg'il côs by "not-tired-enough to sleep".
  • Modifier–adjective order: everything after the adjective, though a rogue adverb may illegitimately tmete and digest an initial adjective.
  • Adjective + argument + adverbial together — untmeted or clean tmesis: degree, argument, adverbial; dirty tmesis: degree, adverbial (nest layer 2), argument.
  • Adverbial phrase, operational definition: that which can strand a polarity clitic.
  • Adverbial-modifiers: 'ya, 't/'thte, aoa, oril, roïl, or…il, ro…il, and double-negative-modified adverbs (aoa niri'thte "not justn't" = "just about": aoa niri'thte ios thurn). Adverbs modify adverbials via this same set.
  • PPs modify adverbials with by, lending through-ness and a process underway: lus Owis malach by "the night late through".
  • No official cases; by can drop for bare measure-nouns.
  • Adverbial clauses on adverbials: chained sentences, reduced and unreduced clefts — slee'ya iy mæ silgá "faster than you think"; Thas slee mæ silgá yiosnÿ slee'ya'ma atá "What you think fast is even faster" (-'ya'ma = "even x-er").
  • Modifier–adverbial order: degree before, clause after; degree wraps the adverb when tmeted.
  • Several modifiers on one adverbial: stack freely, tmetable — yail ro'arch'il sót by or ya'ro'arch'il'il sót by "much too far to walk". by = as-a-process-or-motion-undergone.
  • Prepositional phrase, operational definition: that which fronts with thénu or tháxya "whom".
  • A preposition may govern anything but the subject (certain fragment-types have the subject act on itself — open).
  • Prepositions without arguments: yes — orphaned clitics, free adverbs, compounds (tmesis nests, suffixes, prefixes, portmanteaus).
  • Prepositions with multiple arguments: Oob • Lus Math o Claim o Gloiyornugh —'ya: Pulsc yiæn ("Dust lies under the mat, curtain, and TV").
  • P-modifiers: clitics, double-negatives, and drú "direct", łix "exact", aomeal "straight"; adverbs freely modify Ps.
  • PPs modify Ps: Oob lus Phailé fæ dræao nhœme ("Under the bed fæ from comes").
  • Measure-NPs modify Ps and trail the PP: Ythús Urgea mêdhië "past the fence by a metre" (mêdhe "metre" → mêdhië "by-a-metre").
  • The clefted-P frame (the one adverbial-clause construction on P): Nirïë yinÿ sin e niyail e łix iostá Ornbeíl "just at* that moment" (*singularly and exactly 'at' the 'at' in question).
  • P-modifier order: drú/łix/aomeal most commonly before P, but sit as happily in the cleft before de-clefting iostá; measure-adverbials trail the PP; cleft-chains ride inside the y-wrap.
  • Noun phrase, operational definition: the give test — an NP is what gæn can give (preserving tmesis words as Nouns).
  • NP-tail adverbials: lus Thrugh ios thurn "the train there" — sís/ios thurn as postposed deictic pairs.
  • NP-level comparative: Niïl holdhmÿa Fæ'hín-iy'ær "a man taller than fær" — head Noun tmesized into the standard-pronoun, iy hanging by the tmeter. Superlative and equative pending.
  • Multiple modifiers: proximity by relevance, English-style order the tie-breaker — a red big old car; tmesized, a re'bi'o'car'ld'g'd. Tmeting optional.
  • One article slot upheld: lus/niïl/possessives share it (*sís lus barred; mhís e Wíc, never *lus mhís e Wíc); in existentials the possessive becomes the article — Mis Scale mith gæn "there is a car".

Coordination

  • ax = but — one word, contrast and correction alike.
  • Placement: o "and" — before the last and/or between every pair; œr "or" — between every pair; ax — between every pair; æxó "and/or" — before the last and/or between every pair; aoæx "either-or-neither" — patterns as æxó; aoœr "neither-nor" completes the family.
  • O, the list-initial 'all' modifier: O Cafi œr Sî "Coffee or Tea" — options offered, both exist, soft both-at-once; connects only o / œr / ax (O cruel ax fair), never æxó/aoæx.
  • The n−1 pattern (between every pair) is obligatory for œr and ax, a licensed variant for o, æxó, aoæx; the single-coordinator-before-the-last pattern belongs to o, æxó, aoæx only.
  • Coordination is category-blind: the family coordinates clauses, NPs, APs, verbs alike.
  • Parallelism: function-parallelism only — whatever fills the same slot may coordinate, whatever the form. Adjective with participle / state-phrase / gerund; Noun with nominalisation; adverbial types with one another. (Participles not yet in canon — dependency flagged.)
  • Voice-mixing under coordination: liberal — a shared subject licenses any voice-mix; subject-identity is the only law.
  • No categorical verb-coordination bans — each conjunct bonds back wherever the syntax coheres; Danæam builds adaptive sentence openers (xyz-xy-xy-x-P:x-o-P:y-o-P:z-o-P:x), including return to an earlier layer; hearers attach clauses by judgment.
  • Ellipsis under identity: the second conjunct may drop a repeated be (the tmetic-fragment precedent extends). Echo-gapping declined — retro-repeaters are questions-only.
  • Polarity clitics never omit: every conjunct declares its own pole; no inherited negation across o.
  • Omission within coordinated NPs / AdjPs / AdvPs: gap or uain, as the resonance serves — the hearer's reach resolves the bare gap; uain stands in as the body. Applies at all three levels alike.

Negation (record-level)

  • No negation-coordinator attraction: "Œr 'thte" is a clipped fragment (coordinator + bare pole as a full utterance), not a nor; aoœr = neither-nor.
  • Neg-raising is the default: Ten yiosnÿ fæ nhœmea silgá'thte — the pole on the matrix verb reads either way; docking the pole low disambiguates (…nhœmea'thte silgá); aoa may wilfully re-ambiguate. Double negatives are disembodiers ("not theren't" = thereabouts), never reinforcers.

Anaphora

  • Zero anaphora: zero subjects licensed where be agrees (más, -s carry enough) — a licence, never a law.
  • Dropping to-be creates an assumed first person — singular by default, plural if secondary cues show it (Threïg. "I'm tired"); second and third person expect at least be.
  • sê, the reflexive: reaches its reporter in reported speech, not confined to S'aige'in. Other anaphora belongs to fæ / í / cía.
  • No special anaphoric pronoun — the settled personal pronouns suffice; the gender-design guards something undisclosed and won't tolerate one. Cultural note: the Síonæyais broadly reject the premise of multiple genders, insisting on fæ regardless of the subject's preference.
  • Other anaphoric means: the six-cell text-deixis set — iosiyao "farther back" · iosaoya "farther along" · sísiyao "nearer back" · sísaoya "nearer along" · sísiya "nearer" · iosiya "farther" (back = the already-said; along = the about-to-be-said).
  • Anaphora is direction-free; the text-deixis set rides atop as optional precision.
  • Within the clause: clause-mates disjoint — fæ…fæ are two fæs; coreference takes the reflexive: sê-fær "færself", naked sê "myself" (second person unruled). fær stands last in the compound — an adjective, taking the in-lieu-of-tmesis position.
  • Across coordinate structures: free, either direction across o.
  • Between main and subordinate clauses: free, both orders, both directions — Fæ yiosnÿ fæ ærmuch atá saige "fæ said fæ was tired"; fronted y-clauses may carry the pronoun before its Noun.
  • Between sibling subordinates: free, left-to-right as everywhere; pronouns stack through three clauses (fæ…fæ…fæ) — no siblinghood fence.
  • Between sentences: ordinary, no horizon fence (In As í nhœme attested).
  • Next to complementisers: transparent — no positional sanctuaries anywhere; y- and ios cast no shadow.

Reflexivity

  • Reflexivity is pronominal: sê — invariable, nonclitic, a free word; compounds as sê-fær. No variable form; person shown by the frame (naked sê = 1st, sê-fær = 3rd, 2nd unruled). No verbal reflexive affix; nothing beyond sê bears the office.

  • Not clause-bound: sê is logophoric, reaching its reporter across the clause boundary.

  • All affixal-reflexive subquestions: n/a — no affixal reflexive exists.

  • Reflexive placement: free — the reflexive stands where resonance and speaker please; no positional law.

  • The paradigm: sê · í-sê "itself" · sê-fær "færself" · miths-sê "ourselves" — bases are the object series; every antecedent pairs with its own compound, no exotic binding conditions. fær stands last (the adjective's in-lieu-of-tmesis seat).

  • Reflexives inside nominalisation: the eës-split gerund — Eë'shrai-shrai's sê-fær "the-praising-of færself"; no English "fær Xing færself" scaffold.

  • Reflexives in ordinary NPs: freely — no binding cage was ever built; picture-nouns don't leak; the e-construction (pictures e X-sê) is available signposting, not required.

  • Antecedent-less reflexive: naked sê reads "myself" (1st person only).

  • Other uses of sê: emphatic — yes. No detransitivising verb-form exists. Alone-duty (sg) and reciprocal "each other" (pl): the alone-construction rides mé — wil cías-sê mé "all by themselves" (a plural lonely "all alone"); wil'ya cías-sê mé reassures the listener.

Comparison

  • Comparison = comparative element + particle: mÿa (the 'ma+'ya weld) on the compared term, iy introducing the standard — NP or clause alike, always bare (no case, no P-road): Holdhmÿa iy tens; slee'ya iy mæ silgá.
  • No standard-only comparison in canon — mÿa attends every attested comparative. The degree circumfixes (Or…il / Ro…il) scale degree but do not compare to a standard.
  • Comparative-clause ellipsis: everything sheds but the standard-NP — the reduced clause is the unmarked form; a clause standard sheds its repeated pieces all the way down to the name.
  • iy never omits. Shedding the rival altogether means shedding the frame too: Holdhmÿa. stands as an allowed solitary fragment; *Holdhmÿa iy. is absurd — iy never stands without its standard.
  • The repeated adjective must vanish; its copula may stay: iy lus Sídia ___ (atá). The degree speaks once.
  • Two comparative structures, distinct by syntax, one mÿa in both: predicative with free iy (Holdhmÿa iy tens) vs attributive with the head Noun tmesized into its standard, iy riding the tmeter (Niïl holdhmÿa Fæ'hín-iy'ær).
  • iy + NP is a bare phrase — no silent structure; clausehood exists exactly where y-marking shows it (iy + y-clause).
  • Correlative comparison: the sin…sy hinge (sin opens, sy answers): sin aernmÿa sy sainmÿa "the hotter, the better". aern "hot" · sain "good". Both mÿa-forms written solid, seam-less.

Possession (record-level)

  • Alienability rides the donor: mith gæn (we-give) = inalienable, í gæn (it-gives) = alienable — Mis Glaoh-Glaoh tes Sáinsu mé teo mith gæn / Lus Glaoh-Glaoh tes Sáinsu mé teo í gæn.
  • Tenure: plain gæn is undefined for tenure and the more common; the marked pair — gænyin (temporally bound) · gilæn (eternally regiving).
  • The naming-split (cross-cutting persons/animals/things): unnamed possessa take the prenominal possessive; the uniquely named take the postposed absolute, hyphen-hung on the name (faer 26th dormitory vs Dormitory 26-faers) — extends to ours and theirs. The one-door law explains it: names have no door, so possession hangs on behind. Open cells: the absolute series beyond færs; the ours-cell presumably still bows to the ours-deferral.
  • Tense across the gæn family: past -ea, future ië- — gænea o iëgæn o aich gænyinea o iëgænyin o aich gilænea o iëgilæn. gilæn = g'[w]il'æn: wil "all" tmesized into gæn, the hard w paid, welded solid. aich "likewise".

Emphasis (record-level)

  • No emphasis by stress or accent — deafness is the law: the Síonæyais shout and stress freely, but irrespective of meaning; clitics and order do the work.
  • No emphasis-movement in statements (initial, final, preverbal, or otherwise). Order-shifting lives uniquely in answers to questions, and there it is answerhood mechanics, not emphasis: to "A ghost!?" — A ghost'ya / Ghost — yes / Yes. Ghost'ya.
  • Clefting serves declarative emphasis: Ios nhœme í John atá "It's John that came." Sentence-opening clefters stand bare — no y…nÿ.
  • Pseudo-clefting, two grades — full: Sin uain ythénÿ nhœme ios í John Faevreau atá "The one who came is John Faevreau" (mid-sentence relative wears its y…nÿ wrap: ythénÿ); clipped: Thé nhœme ios John Faevreau atá (sentence-opening, bare).
  • The kit's asymmetry (law): affirmatives double across carriers — bháya'ya; fith'ya bha (clitic on the verb + clipped bha trailing); negatives chain their one morpheme only — fith't o 't o 't, the bare 't riding the coordinator. Chained negation emphasises; stacked negation disembodies. Chain-length unruled.
  • Combinations: the cleft may carry the doubling within (Ios nhœme í John atá'ya); kit × itself is gradable and open-ended — each link adds a degree (shown on the negative chain; higher affirmative stacks unexemplified).
  • The triarchy: constituents → the cleft; polarity → the kit; verbs → repetition. Declarative verb-emphasis is plain repetition after the verb — Fær mæ łathn! Łathn! — where questions echo it before. Whether the verb-repeat grades like the chain: unruled.
  • The decleft doctrine: the cleft is not declefted except manually with iostá (deployed against excessive ambiguity); generally declefting is implicit, signified by atá — every will-be/is/was in a sentence that has hosted a cleft at any point takes iëatá/atá/atáea, even well after the cleft's end (a cleft may end on gæn with the external be still atá). Emphasis docks freely inside the cleft: …gæn — gæn('ya) — atá…. If the cleft itself needs emphasis: kill it and resummon it as a deeply nested tmesis-word. Finite y-clause internals presumably take the same local docking — unconfirmed.
  • The resumptive-í frame (NP emphasis): the emphasised NP stands first, í follows reflecting it, and the sentence closes in the general be — never atá, for no ios stood: Rîbha-e John í cía craigea bhie "John's book, they took it." Pied-pipes without trouble: Rîbha-e John í cía oob lus Phailé dræao craigea bhie. Ending such a sentence with atá is common but proscribed — the atá-family is a sentence's memory of a cleft. NP-internals are reached only by pied-piping the whole NP. craig "take", past craigea.
  • Conjunct emphasis is a polarity act: the kit-clitic docks on the glowing conjunct in situ — X'ya o Y "X indeed, and Y"; Cafi'ya o Sî. No new machinery; one conjunct may glow alone. The whole tetrad presumably docks conjunct-wise (X't o Y; split-pole X'ya o Y't) — unconfirmed; whether a lone conjunct may enter the resumptive-í frame: moot beneath the no-movement law.
  • Multiple constituents may glow at once, each wearing its own 'ya: X'ya … Y'ya. A negative glow wears its pole inside the stamp — 'tya / 'thteya — so added negators must be avoided (stacking poles slides toward double-negation's attenuation): mixed X'ya … Y'tya; split-pole coordination X'ya o Y'tya, not bare 't. Two stamps ruled; higher multiplicities presumed. Ranking among stamps unruled; an 'ma-stamp ('maya) unattested.
  • The two-tier system: movement proper leaves nothing behind (like wh-fronting); dislocation leaves the resumptive — í (things) / fæ (persons). No copies, no particles; r.r.s are agrammatical residents thrown anywhere, not grammatical residues.
  • The one copy-like colloquialism: Fith-fith: ten fith "I am eating" (vs Ten fith, ambiguous between trait and present act) — first person only; formal equivalent Ten dús Fith-Fith más "I toward-the eating am". In other persons it becomes unpleasantly ambiguous and is ruled out.
  • Yes–no question focus: the standing kit — polarity clitics dock on the questioned constituent (Eg'ya / Eg't). Docking within the scér-frame presumed — unconfirmed.

Topic (record-level)

  • Three-plus devices: S'air'n X "as per the taking note of X" (Sin + air by standard tmesis) · O • o e X • (the conversational shifter — an elliptical optative, "May what follows concern X", wearing native imperative-family bullets) · Efui • X: (the explicit announcement; Efui "topic") · the for-its-part replica (s'efui-e'in), placed after its topic · the bare Sin X, clause-initial, running bare — the plainest road; S'air'n does not supersede it.

  • Closing boundary per device: Efui • X: colon · O • o e X • closing bullet · S'air'n X bare · Sin X bare.

  • No bare topic-movement. First-person exception: the smoosh — a single elided o serves as the two os and the O, the closing • splitting out to the up-trill ∆: O Scóhn ∆ ten mac "And John — I like." First-person-only, optional — the first person's third colloquial license beside Más-ten and Fith-fith: ten fith. The ∆ is a segmental vocable at the topic boundary, not sentence-tune; the deafness law unbreached. Scóhn = John nativised; mac "like".

  • The topic-marker rides parenthetically: Niïl Fhós — si'te'rhié'os-e'n — o ten duín Łif elfæn bhie "A bitch — as of my mother — and I hope in Hell." Position free; the single clause-final bhie closes over the wedged material — the architecture deliberately loose. Fhós "bitch" · Rhié "parent" · duín "in (to-at)" · Łif "Hell" · elf "hope (n.)" · elfæn "hope (v.)".

  • No final-seat topic: the clause ends on its be. Afterthoughts are fresh clauses pointing at the former (model: Œr 'thte "Or not"), never movement — so heavy-shift can never be misread as topicalisation.

  • The seam-law of topic placement: the topic-parenthetical rides anywhere, but only at the seams — every licit slot stands at a major-constituent boundary, never phrase-internal (not inside an NP, a before-phrase, or the verb-complex): Air • Mis ssai Grainê "s'aige'n" aoíth dulagá mith iësulæn "Note!: our two graves (as said) before embarking we will-dig." The seam after Air • re-strikes its •. The s'X-e'in dress is productive (s'ulfœrth-e'in beside s'efui-e'in); the free shape sin e X stands beside it, dash-bounded; s'aige'n shows the 'in → 'n elision runs freer than suspected. ssai "two" · Grainê "grave(s)" · aoíth "before" · dulagá "embark(ing)" · sulæn "dig" (iësulæn "will-dig") · Ulfœrth "revenge-journey". Open: mith comitative-or-homonym; the quote-marks' status; Grainê's number after ssai.

  • Marker splitting on the dislocation tier: topic-dislocation wears a topic-marker; emphasis-dislocation rides bare — one tier, two dresses, the marker disambiguating. The thinnest marker (the smooshed O … ∆) suffices, and beneath it the resumptive itself may elide — O Te'fhó'rhié's'os ∆ ten laíea "Bitch-mother-mine, I saw [her]" — the seat warm but invisible; whether an overt í/fæ may also stand there: open. laíea "saw" (past of laí). Te'fhó'rhié's'os: the te…'os kin-frame's first compound guest; the 's before 'os — compound-linker or migrated coda — watched.

  • Initial dislocation = the first seam, after any sentence-particles. No separate contrastive-topic mechanism: glow reaches a topic only through the tmesis-fold road — a tmesis-word swallowing the clause with 'ya flagged on the folded word's tail (full exemplar awaited).

  • No dislocation-proper to final position. The clarification-tail is a topic-reminder behaving as any parenthesis on the post-clausal seam: Niïl sain Hín fæ bhie — mao Rhié "A good man he is — your mother." Pointing fragments are free in form and may carry orphaned clitics. sain "good".

  • Dislocation at any seam, per the seam-law and parenthesis-equivalence; never inside a phrase. The r.r. alone stays outside the law.

  • No topic agreement: agreement's only faces are subject-bound (más 1st person, plural -s, the courtesy-bhies); nothing tracks topichood.

  • No other topic-marking means beyond the ruled family; intonational means barred by the deafness law.

  • Combinations: two markers may stack on one topic, and a marker combines with the for-its-part replica as reprise — Efui • Sin X; Efui • Scean: … (s'efui-e'in) …. Stacking carries no formality ladder — combinations live unranked.

  • The topic-licence is í's sayable range: what gæn can give, í can say, and what í may say can stand as topic; fæ's range rides within by the twin-law (í things, fæ persons).

  • Topicalisable — NPs: yes, the core case, dress intact; gerund-NPs by NP-hood. Whether indefinite niïl-phrases make poor topics: unruled.

  • Adjectives: not as themselves, predicative or attributive — the door is nominalisation.

  • Verbs: not as verbs — the gerund door stands open (the reduplication Nouns).

  • Adverbials: not per type — a nominal of the circumstance (Ornbeíl "moment" and kin) enters by the Noun door; the deictic adverbs (sís/ios thurn) are demonstrative-built, not í-resumable — outside the licence.

  • Main-clause constituents: yes — objects attested, and whole main clauses by the riding sin e X. Base order is OSV, so subjects sit medial except in first person; the marker makes a subject-topic audible regardless of seat.

  • Subordinate constituents: freely, at any depth, finite or nonfinite — resumption is anaphora. The path leaves a trace by depth: the first subordinate clause is un-y-marked; the second and beyond are y-marked but never nÿ-marked. The question-path wears the full seal, the topic-path the bare prefix. The riding sin-e-X topic, seatless, leaves no path. Collision of the two y-offices in one sentence: watched.

  • NP-constituents: only by whole-NP pied-piping — the part never travels alone.

  • Coordinate constituents: no — the coordinate island holds whole; conjuncts travel together or not at all, and in-situ dressing fails — every topic-marker does double duty (Sin as "as", o as "and"). The one wall anaphora may not pierce. The frame is the resumptive í: it resumes, not reflects.

  • Multiple topics at once: yes, so long as every secondary topic takes the object position — one primary topic (the shared subject) spoken once and elided thereafter; secondaries stacked in order, each the object of its own predicate; the predicates follow in matching first-to-first order, verb-final discipline making the pairing legible. A referent may be topicalised twice, each visit earning its own predicate. No single-topic law.

  • Movement is never involved in topic: topics ride as parentheticals born at their seams, or stand in the marked dislocation — fronting-with-a-double, not movement. No copies (the first-person Fith-fith exception stands); the dislocation's proform is í/fæ, eliding beneath the topic-marker in the attested exemplar; no particles — nothing moves, so nothing strands; the visible surface shows nothing, by elision.

  • Topicalisation is optional everywhere; degree of preference unranked.

Heavy shift

  • No heavy shift, ever: weight goes to a fresh Aich—sentence (Lus Hín nhœme. Aich— mé-e fær éx Nímhogê eibh "The man came. Also — with him six dogs were") or packs whole into a ythénÿ-relative inside its NP (Lus Hín ythénÿ éx Nímhogê mé-e eibh nhœme), verb-last order absorbing it unmoved. All heavy-shift subquestions have no object. aich "also", the chain-connective · éx "six" · Nímhogê "dog(s)" · mé-e "with-of" opens the accompaniment-existential, a second possession road beside the gæn engine (division of labour watched); the with-phrase survives relativisation with its complement gapped.

Word-class fences

  • Adpositions are invariably preposed (dús, du, yth, ythús, drú, łix, duîm, dræao, by, arch…); no postpositions.
  • Numeral vs quantifier: a numeral counts bare with its Noun in the -ê form (ssai Grainê, éx Nímhogê) — the counted-ê is its exclusive badge; a quantifier governs partitive e (mhís e Wíc). But partitive e is no membership card — e's of-office is general territory: Chair-Chair e Emma "rain of blood"; sís Tïc e Bis "This House of Grief". chair "rain" (via Chair-Chair) · Emma "blood" · Tïc "house" · Bis "grief" · Ruimais "novel".
  • The article class is defined by du-fusibility: lus → dús, niïl → dúïl. Other offered fences (particles the reducible, clitics the orphan-survivors, pronouns the toll-payers) remain true behaviours, unpromoted to definitions.

Syntactic functions & the function-country (record-level)

  • Syntactic functions are expressed by order, prepositions, and clitics — never case: the language has no case morphology.

  • An unexpressed subject is carried by be-agreement and default: más/másas name the 1st person, -s carries number; the wholly naked predicate belongs to the speaker.

  • Indirect object: duîm-fær — one flag, hyphen-dangled onto the pronoun; it may also tmete: fæ'uîm'ær — in the fold the roles invert, the pronoun hosting and the flag guesting, duîm paying its hard toll (d → 'uîm), the host split fæ…ær with the æ seam-doubled (anatomy watched). IO-before-DO order stands attested, unpromoted to law.

  • Object of comparison: iy + bare standard — no case.

  • Object of equation: the sin…sy correlative — sin mordach sy fær "as big as fær"; in practice under OSV the sy-phrase takes the object slot and fronts: sy fær sin mordach fæ bhie. Equation's sin…sy stands apart from comparison's iy — two standards, two markers; the standard wears the object form. mordach "big" · sy "so-then". The superlative alone remains pending.

  • Other verb-governed objects — the standing list: nhœme + dræao; gæn + duîm-X; ghiæan + by; degree + dúe + infinitive; be + mé-e X. Exemplar: Ydúynÿ lus Ti'amár'th tes Meilstory mao Łamis atá "In the ocean of pain my diving suit your saliva is" — with af-/fel- (up/down) variants: felTi'amár'th … afŁamis / afTi'amár'th … felŁamis.

  • The Verbless Eternal Future (that which will always never be going to happen): O þin-e • Hthom-e'ya o Gil • Hth'— Gil:Ti'amár'th-dú'lus'y=Meilstory-teos=Łamis-mæos—'om. þis-e V Hthom't. Gil without 't: with 't the noncreation force would read as not-hers and the sequel merely possible; without, "can, has, does, and will never exist(/ed)". The Verbless Eternal Past (that which will always already have happened): Ton o-e • Hthom-e'ya o Aól'ya • Hth'— Aól:…—'om. Tonu-e ∆ Hthom'ya. Ton/Tonu "he" the creation force, þin/þis "she" the noncreation force — ceremonial pronouns outside the universal-fæ system; Gil and Aól are nouns of the Object-of-(Negative-)Giving's-Force, "gift" and "entry" poetic readings. Machinery: Tith splits Ti…th around amár; Hthom splits Hth'—…—'om around a whole proposition — proposition-sized guests take dash-seams; the =-linker chains verbless predication; -os revealed as the absolute-possessive former — teos "mine", mæos "yours" (mao → mæ at the seam); Hthom't "worldn't" — the kit as this register's only verb-force; Ydúynÿ the y-marked in-word, co-occurring with atá per law.

  • Become's complement wears the toward-series: Duílúch Fæ'du'ærs fæ ghiæan — the goal-state du-marked. Whether a bare complement also lives (ghiæan as a seventh be-coat): unruled.

  • Subject-complement stands bare — no dús, no e: Ten Væx farmea eibh "I was made king" / Væx fæ eibh farmea; vs become: Ten Xæxr ghiæanea "I became king" / Xæxr fæ ghiæanea. A passive shape attested before voice-country opens — participle + be, its seat person-asymmetric across the exemplars (before be in 1st person, after in non-1st) — watched, not law. Væx "king" · farm "make".

  • Object-complement stands bare in the same dress: Mith fær Væx farmea "we made him king" — S–O–Comp–V, mith fronting by the first-person seat rule, the complement owning the pre-verb chair. The becoming-gift alternative: Mith fær-dúe Xæxr ghiæanea — the person wears dúe, postposed and hyphen-joined; the -r state-noun rides as object; ghiæan takes causative duty by frame alone. Xæxr travels with ghiæan; farm takes bare Væx.

  • Adjective-governed objects: the standing estate — P-stock trailing (fær uperya e; gáns by), tmesis-compounds, one argument per adjective. férás-ufsae "boy-crazy" fully glossed: Férás "boy" (casual sense: sexual tops and/or dominants — an agender culture, gendered sub- and minority cultures notwithstanding), ufsae "insane" (deliberate ae, /aɪ/). aoyth "like" — a preposition, the vacancy-blocker: …aoyth rhié-færs "like his father"; no like-adjective need exist. The familiar possessive: [kin-noun]-[absolute-possessive] — rhié-færs "parent-his", sídia-teos "sibling-mine" — the kin-noun decapitalised (the everted frame keeps its swallowed lowercase; the toll is not refunded). sídia "sibling" — context renders sister or brother. The different-word still waits.

  • Passive agent: a dræao-source; the passive-with-agent speaks in the ghiæan-frame: Dræao lus Ufín Ooraithr fæ ghiæanea "By the enemy fæ was killed". dræao the will; by the channel. O before S in the non-first person. Ufín "enemy" · Ooraithr "killed-being" (ooraith + -r, patient-flavoured). Whether the farmea-eibh shape takes the same dræao-agent: presumed, unattested — watched.

  • Topic's morphological expression is the marker family itself: the compounds S'air'n and productive s'X-e'in; the marks • and ∆ and the per-device boundary punctuation — punctuation carrying grammar. No case, no agreement.

  • The absolute construction is usually a topic clause, the familiar possessive as its epithet-vehicle: Státachaí-færs "His Idiocy" (in-clause lowercase; the construction presupposes the bond is already known). Attested pair: O státachaí-færs V sê-fær fæ ooraith "His Idiocy, himself he killed" (unkind — the V colours it) · O státachaí-færs ∆ sê-fær niïl Pat fæ sulænea "His Idiocy, færself a hole fæ dug" (amused — the ∆). The trills' affect-split reaches syntax. The seat rule holds universally; the compound is sê-fær (fær last — an adjective, the in-lieu-of-tmesis position); naked sê never 3rd. ooraith "kill" · Státachaí "idiocy" (Stát → Státach → Státachaí) · Státr "idiot-being" · Pat "hole" · sulæn "dig".

  • The infinitive: the toward-prefix bound to the verb — u- (= du-): usót "to go"; the infinitive's subject rides a dúe-phrase; goals may prefix likewise (ułiuafth "Heavensward"; Łiuafth "heaven", the Noun small inside the complex). Plain: Dúe sê ułiuafth usót…; more likely a topic-read: S'łiuafth-e'in — dúe sê S'eg'in usót… — the goal fronted in the as-frame, resumed in-clause by S'eg'in "such-way" (eg the way-element; class open). The du→u reform: some du-prefixes become bare u-; which ones — unresolved, watched. Bare sê here = "the-self", the unmarked generic.

  • Nominalisation ("John's refusal of the offer"): by reduplication with the eës-split genitive frame — the gerund X-X inside opened eës: Eë'shrai-shrai's faer-sê. Fuller nominalisation typology open.

  • The nonlocal-function kit: duîm, mé, by, e, In, the du/u-series, Sin, aoyth, dræao, the gæn-frame — each function-cell picks from the kit.

  • The without-word: æœá, the object-no; æœá-e "of-no, without" — Ten æœá-e Fía más "I am without food"; Æœá-e Fía fæ is. The second seats is as a be-face beside bhies — division or free variation, open. The from/without twins both breathe: dræao and æœá-e.

  • Benefactive: dúe — Ten dúe Piell í átea "I did it for Piell"; the recipient stands apart on duîm — Ten duîm Piell í gæn "I gave it to Piell". Doing-for never collapses into giving-to. át the pro-verb. dúe's offices now three: purposive linker (dúe fith), infinitive-subject flag (dúe sê), benefactive (dúe Piell). Piell the exemplar-name.

  • Source rides dræao (fæ dræao nhœme); the finer flavours ("wool from a sheep" — material-source and kin) stand open.

  • Circumstance ("a man with dirty hands"): relative packing — Niïl Hín ythénÿ reinach Sáinsu mé-e atá… — mé-e trailing its NP, atá by cleft-kinship: the hands stand as a temporary second subject, their being-with served. The negative, three roads: the same with aoa atá, or atá'thte, or restructured — Niïl Hín thé æœá-e reinach Sáinsu bhie… — no cleft, no temporary second subject: the dirty hands don't become a subject because their action, being nonexistent, isn't served; existence earns subjecthood, nonexistence is denied it, and the be-choice (atá vs bhie) tracks it. æœá-e leads its NP (determiner-blood) where mé-e trails (preposition-blood). reinach "dirty" (base rein unminted — watched) · thé the no-cleft relativiser. Gentle fork: plain Adj–N (reinach Sáinsu) beside fær-last — post-head seat particular to pronoun-compounds, or one law awaiting reconciliation?

  • The possession splits are never NP-internal — they live in the engine: alienability in the donor (mith gæn / í gæn), tenure in the verb pair (gænyin / gilæn), time in the engine's tense (gænea / iëgæn). NP-internal marking tracks naming alone.

  • The possessed Noun bears no marking (mis Scale: Scale unmarked — the one-door law); no possessed-marking exists to distinguish alienability or anything else.

  • Quality, three co-valid roads: (1) e serves both bearings — Hín e Gree "a man of humour" fronts the quality, lus Gree e lus Hín "the humour of the man" reverses it; order alone tells them apart. (2) -ach derives the quality straight onto the noun: Grëach Hín "a humoured man" (Gree + ach contracts the double vowel to ë — whether that contraction is the general rule: open). (3) Negative quality inherits the circumstance apparatus unchanged — aoa atá, atá'thte, or thé + æœá-e + bhie; æœá-e Gree "without humour". Gree "humour" · Grëach "humorous" · Grëachaí "humorousness".

  • Quantity, one frame: Q e N — Niïl Scál e thíthíl Tonnaê "a boat of a thousand tons" (the numeral+counted-ê phrase itself filling e's complement; the numeral absorbs one layer of of); Niïl Lund e Shar "a pound of sugar". Scál "boat" · thíthíl "thousand" · Tonna "ton" · Lund "pound (weight)" · Shar "sugar".

  • Material takes by, not e — means and matter merge: Tïc by Úrsapê "a house of bricks / built of bricks". Contrasts (not material): Tïc e Úrsapfarmaí "House of Brickmakery" — e reserved for abstract/industry association; Tïc dúe Úrsap-Farm-farm "House for Brickmaking" — the purposive dúe fronting a hyphen-compound gerund. Two forks resolved: -ê is simply the pluraliser, not a numeral-exclusive badge (the numeral's own -ê is the same plural); and -ach is skipped when it would be absurd — Úrsapfarmea "brick-made" is sayable, *Úrsapfarmach is not; the language reaches straight past the impossible stage to -aí. Úrsap "brick".

  • Manner, three co-valid roads: (1) by Elin ghuethnea "served with verve" — the by-instrumental, unified across material and manner alike; (2) tmesis — El'huethnea'in: Elin splits at el'|'in, hosting ghuethnea toll-paid to huethnea, the verb embedded inside the manner-word (whether Elin's -in tail is kin to Sin's family: a light watch); (3) elinië ghuethnea "vervely served" — Elin + -ië, noun-to-adverb. The negative ("without verve"): unruled, open. Elin "verve" · ghuethn "serve".

  • Cause is a small family, four metaphors for one English preposition: origin-genitive — Ærmuchaí e Síulnê "exhaustion of wounds" (e's of-office reaches cause); gift-given — fær Síulnê fær Ærmuchaí gænea "his wounds gave his exhaustion"; gift-received-from — fæ Ærmuchaí fær Síulnê dræao ghiæanea "he got exhaustion from his wounds" (dræao the source, the fuller ghiæan-frame); destination-arrived-at — Yth fær Síulnê fæ duín Ærmuchaí tugea eibh "over his wounds he was brought to exhaustion" (duín marking the state arrived at). Verb-shaped cause over a plain passive: Yth ír Ralxú lus Both rüstea eibh "the city was ruined by its government" — yth fronting the cause-phrase. by never entered the cause family. No default ruled among the gift-shapes. Light fork: ír "its" beside fær — an inanimate/animate possessive split. Ærmuch "exhausted" · Ærmuchaí "exhaustion" · Síuln "wound" (pl. Síulnê) · Ralxú "government" · Both "city" · rüst "ruin" · tug "bring".

  • Purpose rides dúe/dué "to-of" before the infinitive (or'raif'il—'t dúe fith "hungry enough to eat") and the low road by (côs by "to sleep"). The dúe/dué accent-fork stands unresolved, gently.

  • Function ("used the stick as a club"): the sin…sy equative stretched from adjective-degree to noun-function — Ten sy Cthug sin lus Acusthia lynœa "I used the stick as a club": Ten fronts by the seat rule, the sy-phrase lands in the object slot the rule already names, sin resumes with the true object, the verb closes. Not new grammar — two standing rules meeting. Open note: lynœa spells its past with œ, not plain -ea — possibly a vowel-harmony variant, unconfirmed. Cthug "club" · Acusthia "stick" · lyn "use".

  • Reference ("told him about the incident"): e — Ten fær e Atruens'ya saíea — fær bare as the told-object, e carrying the reference relation. By-promotion and the saige-satellite both declined. e's of-office now spans quality, quantity, and reference; material and cause are the two provinces it doesn't reach. Atruens "incident" · saí "tell".

  • Essive ("in Berlin as a soldier"): the possessed mé — Ten duín Berolth tes-mé Maghr eibh "I was in Berlin my-with soldier-being": tes hyphen-joins mé into tes-mé — accompaniment made possessed. Maghr = Magh + the -r state-former; essive roles join become-states under one nominaliser. Second attestation — the functive: duín tes-mé Matthsyr "as a teacher" — the construction is productive. Berolth "Berlin" · Magh "soldier" · Matthsy "teacher".

  • Translative ("appointed him as general"): same structure as made-king — Mith fær-dúe Cáithoorhr oothíthea: the postposed hyphen-joined fær-dúe flag, an -r state-noun object, the causative verb closing. Two attestations now (ghiæan for becoming, oothíth for appointing) — a real generalising construction, not one lexeme's frame. Translative and become share one structure, undivided. Cáithoor "general" · oothíth "appoint".

  • The partitive family: e presides; inside the e-complement the definite system is five-way (lus does not appear): iocías "those" (ios+cías), sícías "these" (sís+cías), or -'ya "the". Full paradigm: Atruens'ya "the incident" (sg, enclitic) · ios Atruens "that" · sís Atruens "this" · sícías Atruensê "these" · iocías Atruensê "those" · Atruensê'ya "the incidents". Demonstratives front the noun and pluralise with cías; the neutral definite -'ya rides after, plurality marked on the noun (-ê). The by-partitive (paths through wholes): left open.

  • Partitive numeral: the enclitic -'ya is the default — ssai e Frælithi'ya "two of the children". Frælithi is an irregular plural of Fræli "child" — pluralisation is not uniformly -ê; some nouns take their own shape.

  • Nonpartitive numeral: bare and unmarked — ssai Grainê; with possessive, Mis ssai Grainê (Possessive · Numeral · Noun), no mutation.

  • Partitive quantifier: [quantifier] e (lus) [Noun] — mhís e Wíc the attested type. The scale's missing middle (a some-word between ni and yail) is a lexicon gap, not a grammar gap.

  • Partitive negative quantifier — a five-dress scale, all riding partitive e + the 'ya definite: niuain e Frælithi'ya "fewer-than-one of the children" · aoa uain e Frælithi'ya "not one of the children" · æouain e Frælithi'ya "none of the children" (the object-no welded to the prop-one) · ssíns e Frælithi'ya "zero of the children" (ssíns — zero joins the numerals) · and the existential road — æœá Frælithi'ya í gænyin "there were no children": æœá direct on the NP, the dummy í giving — nonexistence denied subjecthood. niuain · æouain "none" · ssíns "zero".

  • Nonpartitive negative quantifier: æœá direct on the NP (beside the adnominal of-dress æœá-e); the existential wears gænyin with dummy í.

  • Price rides dúe: dúe ssai Lundê "for two pounds" — the weight-pound is Lund; the currency-pound is Lyr.

  • Value: the descriptive e-frame, quality's own office. The worth-relative attested: …niïl Borhu yiosnÿ duîm niïl Eœd e cliff Lyrê atá… "a table worth five pounds" — yiosnÿ the y-relative taking atá by the y-law. Borhu "table" · Eœd "worth" · cliff "five" · Lyr "pound (currency)".

  • Distance: a bare measure-NP adverbial, no preposition ("chased him a mile").

  • Extent: quantity's frame extended — N e measure (+ adjective): "a building of a mile high".

  • Concessive: wythil ‹ yth + wil — the all-word welded over the past-word ("despite").

  • Inclusion: metałie "inclusive-of", trailing its NP — Wilhíæ yScéan metałie "everyone including Scéan"; y- serves as the special subordinate marker for proper nouns. Wilhíæ "everyone".

  • Exclusion: most commonly a negated y-clause — Wilhíæ yScéan aoa atáea "everyone except Scéan". The y-law: the y summons the atá family — thé (no y) takes bhie; ythénÿ, yiosnÿ, yScéan (y) take atá; atáea attests the family bending with -ea.

  • Addition: the aich (sin) chain — "three people, John also-as".

  • Vocative: trills, not a particle — up-energy ∆, down-energy V; the fæ majority express them vocally; obligatoriness unruled. Riders: the bare-noun clause-initial custom stands; Scéan-teos available in intimacy (the familiar possessive as vocative); the communal existential — O mis Scéan mith dúe silgá gæn "And there is Scéan to think of". silgá "think".

  • Citation form: the bare lemma, Nouns capitalised. Quote-nesting: top layer >– –<, lower layers »– –«.

  • Labels and shop-fronts: bare NPs under the fragment-licence — the licence's paradigm case.

  • The local-functions table: at-rest = In; motion-to = dúe (bare du-family); motion-from = dræao; motion-past = by — with duîm, mé, duín, usót beside, and the directions duáf "upward" · ufel "downward" · dúy "to-through" · uÿth "to-over".

  • Time: In is THE time-locative, category-blind; the du-family trio — dúiri "until" · drúræao "since/after/subsequent-from" · duílín "within [all-in]" — plus duáo "ago" (+ measure); measures ride bare. In + [name] covers periods of day, weekdays, months, years, festivals, seasons — the names are uncoined (watched). Frequentative: In + plural (In X-ê "on Mondays"). Punctual-future: In + measure ("back in two hours"). Duration: a bare measure-NP ("lived there two years"). Anterior-general: aoíth "before" + time-Noun. Posterior-general: drúræao. Point-in-period: duílín + period, tense-blind.

  • Number: -ê the general pluraliser; irregular plurals live (Frælithi ‹ Fræli); the copula bends (-s: bhies, másas); niïl carries singular meaning; the singular is unmarked.

  • One plural — distributivity is phrasal: Fær Nif e sê-fær Wiunail sulfhœa "everyone blew their nose" — the neutral fæ giving an overt singular-they. Wiunail "everyone" beside Wilhíæ — forked gently; Nif "nose" beside Myth "nose" — flagged.

  • Singulatives: the -r suffix is the unit road for masses — uain Wícr "one water-being".

  • No noun classes, genders, or classifiers anywhere in canon; nothing marks class on numerals, prepositions, or verbs; loanwords (Scale, Thrugh, by, film) simply walk in classless. The pronoun system is deliberately genderless.

  • Definiteness is obligatory: a definite NP must be a proper noun, preceded by lus, or enclitic via 'ya. lus lives.

  • The definite inventory (proper name / lus / 'ya) is category-blind — abstracts included ('ya or lus on abstracts). Proper names take no article — names have no door: possession hangs behind them, and lus/niïl never stand before them. Obligatory there as everywhere.

  • Indefiniteness: niïl is optional; the inventory — niïl, bare plurals, bare mass nouns with the -r unit-road beside; optional throughout. Bare plurals serve, and "there are more suffixes for this than -ê" — the plural family is larger than one suffix: watched. With masses the unit-road is preferred: uain Wícr over niïl Wícr.

  • Referentiality is unmarked — niïl and bare Nouns carry referential and nonreferential readings alike. Genericness is unmarked — the bare Noun is generic.

  • No obviation — third persons are never ranked; í and the existential-we are not obviating; a person may be stressed with 'ya or de-emphasised with 'thte/'t, but that does not change the core pronoun.

  • Free pronouns are not obligatory everywhere: 1st person droppable under más/másas, plural subjects under -s forms; elsewhere the pronoun (or at least be) is expected. Plain noncontrastive use is ordinary; pronouns stand as cleft-focus; no other special conditions.

  • "(It is) I": Más-ten — a tonally casual "I'm", first person singular only; no plural or other-person inversions exist.

  • Emphatic vs unemphatic pronouns: segmental in both directions — emphasis adds (the kit-stamp in situ; sê as emphatic), reduction ingests (the toll-paid faces inside tmesis — fær → 'ær in Fæ'hín-iy'ær — are the reduced pronouns, seam-bound by definition). Suprasegmental difference barred by the deafness law.

  • Pronoun + numeral: mith ssai "we two" is grammatical; no dual needed. No collective/distributive pronoun plurals.

  • No avoidance-grammar of titles-over-pronouns, but honorific style exists: Fær Alcuilaí "Fær Radiance"; in full, Fær Alcuilaí Sê-dúi'gheo-e'ri "Fær Radiance Self-Unto-God-of". An open style, not closed grammar — built on the -aí abstracts and Sê-compound epithets. dúiri "unto" ‹ du + niri.

  • Nonspecific indefinite pronouns (one): the office is held by mith and í, with a conditioned mæ-substitute — when a real 1pl subject occupies a non-subordinate clause, the generic mith yields and mæ substitutes. No dedicated form; no Nouns serve the office. mith substitutes for I and you in neutral questions. Whether fæ-generic is barred by dignity: open.

  • Specific indefinite ("someone"): uain't-aoa — "about one", the double-negative device worn as a chain; plain uain declined.

  • Emphatic pronouns: the standing machinery, no new organ — [pronoun] + kit-stamp by the any-class docking law (mæ'ya and kin), sê as emphatic; degrees by kit-chaining, gradable and open-ended; fully regular — the sê-family by base + -sê across the object series (sê · í-sê · sê-fær · miths-sê · cías-sê).

  • Selective emphatic ("we, but especially I"): Mith ax ten'ya — ax extended to the selective office with the 'ya stress on the singled member.

  • No pronoun–noun same-reference ("we firemen") — instead the mé-apposition: mith Łúgrchainr-mé "we fireman-being-with". Łúgrchain "fireman" ‹ Łúgr "fire".

  • Inclusory constructions live ("we (and) the priest" = "I and the priest"), including with pronoun pairs.

  • Reciprocals: the plural sê-forms — cías-sê (and miths-sê) read "each other" when heads are many, the resonance deciding; not common nouns. The forms distinguish only their base's person; no case; they alone bear the office; the same forms are the plain reflexives, and with mé they carry the plural alone-duty. The 2nd-person plural: unruled.

  • Nonrestrictive relatives are clefts — the relativiser paradigm ythé / ythénÿ // ythés / ythésnÿ.

Voice

  • The personal passive exists, one type: the -ea participle + be (farmea eibh), the patient promoted to subject. The active's direct object becomes the passive subject; the indirect object never does; other constituents may.
  • The impersonal frame exists — the dummy í (the æœá … í gænyin machinery); its passive reach follows the dummy. Formable from DO-verbs with the object retained (át / átea / iëát "will-do"), from other-object verbs, and from objectless verbs — but not from IO-verbs.
  • The agent in the passive: the dræao-phrase; by stays the means-channel.
  • The passive is periphrastic — the be-grid carries it through every tense and aspect (eibh past, iëao the future-face). Dynamic vs static distinguished: dynamic rides the ghiæan-road; static is the eibh-shape.
  • Valency decreases by silence — arguments drop freely under the drop-licence: anticausative by subject-silence ("the water boiled" = boil, subject unspoken); object-drop free and zero-marked (Theváre fæ) — which verbs refuse the drop: watched. No reciprocal intransitive — cías-sê serves; the -self family places pronouns after the word (sê-cías). No other valency-decreasing means attested.
  • Valency increases by the causative construction: fær-dúe + -r state-noun + causative verb (Xæxr ghiæanea; Cáithoorhr oothíthea) — of intransitives, transitives, and ditransitives alike; no formal difference by causee agentivity; the causee omits straightforwardly.
  • No special reflexive or reciprocal verb-forms — both offices are pronominal.

Aspect & mood (record-level)

  • The perfect exists (saige "have spoken" its attestation), in all three tenses — present, pluperfect (Ten stílsótea), future perfect (Iëao As í iëátea) — and the double pluperfect: Ten úteo tes St'sót-sót'íl stílgænea. stíl- is the perfect's former. One form carries all readings — present-result, experiential, begun-and-continuing, just-completed — undistinguished; perfect and recent past are unmarked apart, kinship total.

  • The aspect-inventory: perfective = the bare verb, all verbs; imperfective/continuous = the dús-frame — Ten dús Fith-Fith más "I am eating" (formal), Fith-fith: ten fith (semicolloquial, 1st person only); the stative question — may "is knowing" stand? — unruled, watched. Habitual = the bare present, gnomic. Ingressive = the ghiæan-frame. Terminative = thuíl, four free orders: saige-thuíl · thuísaige · thu'aige'íl · thuíl saige; completion-before-another = an aoíth-clause. Iterative = the tenure-road (gilæn "regives again and again"). Semelfactive, punctual, durative, simultaneous, and telicity: unmarked. Aspect combinations free — no bars, no restrictions with voices, tenses, moods, or nonfinite forms.

  • Intentional mood: the self-vow — Ai'en'lith, narrativemancy: the speaker stories femself into the deed (1sg); other persons unruled.

  • No debitive mood — the normal imperative serves obligation.

  • Potential: the Syá-family (sentence-adverbial possibility); no physical-ability/permission split; no learned-ability form.

  • The certainty ladder: Syá (may be) / bare (is) / ios-emphatic (must be) — assembled from attested pieces, provisional.

  • Monitory: the Mee-frame (provisional).

  • No dedicated narrative mood — the Verbless Eternal register serves the mythic.

  • Consecutive ("therefore"): aich sin "also-as" (provisional). Contingent ("it is possible that"): Syá as sentence-adverbial.

  • Incorporation: objects and pronouns ride into complexes (the tmesis engine); subjects never incorporate, transitive or intransitive; indirect objects may ride in.

  • Absolute vs contingent state in adjectives: yes — tenure-predication.

Comparison & degree morphology

  • The comparative: mÿa on the compared term + iy with the standard. Equality: the sin…sy correlative — equality and inequality never share a word — law. Superlatives: mÿel "more-than-any" and niyel "fewer-than-any", with variant definite articles; in superlative-of-a-set, the partitive e either precedes the article or appends to the adjective-turned-being-noun (Holdhr) — both grammatical; comparison of a thing to itself at other times: yes. No other superlative types but poetic ones.
  • Degree in large measure: sinya "muchly". In small measure ("rather tall"): the attenuator — the double negative disembodies into approximation: aoa [adj]'t / 'thte ("semi-rightward-turn-ness": aoa duíxeis film't) — provisional application.
  • Adverb comparison: equality — sin…sy reaches adverbs; comparative — the -mÿa suffix generalised from adjectives (contrariety wears -ma, the contradiction suffix; slee'ya: degree-reading or doublet — fork); superlative — the same mÿel/niyel mechanism; no other kinds.
  • Adverb degree: "very quickly" rides the degree machinery as litotic boost — Ten í or'mordachië'il—'t craithië fithea "I ate extremely hungrily": the or…il attenuator wraps the degree-word via tmesis, a dash-hung 't negates the whole wrapped proposition, not-insufficiently reading as an intensifier; alternate or'mordachaí-by'il—'t "with-not-insufficient-enormity". "Too quickly" = Ro'[adverb]'il on the same wrap-pattern. craithië "hungrily" (craith- the inferred hunger-root) · fith "eat" (fithea "ate") · mordachië "enormously" · mordachaí "enormity" — extensions of the established mordach "big".

Numerals (record-level)

Derivation (record-level)

  • Adjective → abstract Noun by -aí: Grëachaí "humorousness" · mordachaí "enormity" · Státachaí "idiocy" · Ærmuchaí; the -ach stage skips when semantically absurd. The -aí nouns are noun-like throughout — articles, e, by, case-frames like any Noun; no sentence-like syntax.

  • No nouns from adverbs. Nouns from clauses: canon — the tmesis fold-road makes one Noun of a relative clause (Ië'mal'rugh'ach'nÿ, an NP by the gift test), and Hthom swallows propositions whole. Other categories (particles, pronouns): unattested — watched.

  • Verbs from Nouns by -æn (elf → elfæn), productivity open. Verbs from verbs: the tenure-derivations (gilæn, gænyin). No adjective-to-verb morphology — the ghiæan-frames do the work. None from adverbs; none beyond -æn.

  • Adjectives from verbs: the -ea participle (farmea attributive; Chardea's shape). Adjectives from adjectives: the degree circumfixes — Or…il "not X enough" · Ro…il "too X" — and the comparative -mÿa; an -ish and an un-word remain uncoined, watched. None from adverbs or elsewhere.

  • Adjective → adverb by -ië (mordachië, craithië); elinië the Noun-road cousin. Adverbs from adverbs: no dedicated suffix — the degree/attenuator syntax does it. Noun-ië and Adj-ië stand alone.

  • Complex prepositions form by the weld — wythil, duílín, drúræao, dúiri the four attestations; the possibilities are P+P true compounds (duín ‹ du + In; beside the P+article welds dús, dúïl, ythús) and quantifier-welds (wil into yth; the drú-, du- compounds). Whether P-governing-PP sequences also stand, and whether du is the only fusing root: watched. No nominal ("in front of") or verbal ("depending on") formations — the weld-road serves.

  • No adjectival complex-preposition formations. Article-fusion completes the set: du + lus → dús, yth + lus → ythús.

  • Simple derived prepositions: the loan-road only — none denominal, deverbal, or deadjectival (aoyth is underived); the grubby loans stand as the evidence.

Phonology (record-level)

  • Distinctive segments, allophony (nonsyllabics and syllabics), word-edge consonants, consonant clusters, and syllable structure: outstanding — parked.
  • Loan-flavoured segments: þ and the trill-letter V — register, not class-restriction.
  • Vowels are free at both word-edges and in sequence — the ï seam-law is the one written rule.
  • Roots are word-shaped; only the affix-stock is bound.
  • No systematic unit-restrictions beyond the seam-law and the toll — onsets, codas, cross-syllable combinations all free.
  • Vowel harmony: "yes, essentially" — local suffix-harmony (-as; lynœa's œ), no root-spreading system. No consonant harmony — the seam-and-toll is the only consonant law.
  • No distinctive length anywhere.
  • Stress: no fixed word-accent — stress falls freely and means nothing (the deafness law); no grammatical role, no canonical position. The trills ∆/V can continue alongside speech, sounding through to the sentence's end or the next • — the one exception; humans cannot produce them.
  • No tone: no lexical, grammatical, or partial tone; pitch never bears meaning (scér/scén differ by segment, never pitch); no tonal morphophonology, downstep, or updrift. If the ideophones ever gain melody, the no-tone rulings reopen.
  • Intonation: no canonical peak; no intonation pattern bears syntactic or semantic function — questionhood, emphasis, and topic all travel segmentally. Emphatic and contrastive intonation exist vocally and mean nothing. Interaction with stress and tone: grammatically nil, except the trills running parallel. No segmental effects from intonation; the trills are segmental-parallel vocables (provisional).
  • Stress and tone under morphology: nothing to move, vacuously constant — no accent, no tone. Intonation causes no segmental change.
