E —méstármÿa—,

O glás •

and when back before the earthland the waves flew think you to their ancienter boundaries they fled? And your advance ran clean to nor farther than fairness’ line?

Look: Of floods there were two. Avarice ours. Wrath yours.

Of the uplands no mass greater than afPunóny’s-cum-Hungary’s there lay. And from-neath afPunóny Peak Faerie rose and rose and the valleylands and lowlands and trenchlands to us fell.  And now by the Flood of Wrath were your children to nurseries of our make and our wringing born. And so wrong choices were made of eld.

What-so-ever might the police generals argue, I know, as you will intuit: Such uplings so born to what —of our Faerie— remains are our children. As into each clip of taken ground so-born are our estranged your children. And to us, for our children, the whole responsibility belongs. So far as any —as my clade of waves; ours of littlenesses; even ours of mounds— are the Uplings Faerielanders.

Yet be convinced. By Bryth ufCastucy were these testimonies penned. ‘Woman’ by title, fae authored this furling of the Upling day-to-day so, with a good but soft-attentioned Waver friend, as to share the transpirings of faer fraught tenure in Mionchain. Born a Bródlainny, the riots of 49.43 and faer graduation from Uælniytaí Lódla-e preceded ufCastucy’s relocation to Mionchain. Near six-and-dozen years fae fashioned faerself a Mionchainy and, moreover, stalwart of the Mionchainish Upling Community. UfCastucy took the Sacred Negative as faer pronoun —also: animal feminine— after that same ‘she’ and ‘he’ pattern you wear yourselves.

For people our Faerish gives only the ‘fae’ — yet for animals of semen and the Radiant Creative we give ton (he) and for animals of the ova and Sacred Negative þin (she). And tons and tones. And þis and þon.

When fae of waves of Bryth ufCastucy’s old acquaintance —a one Âhern Amaldéu-e (myself); and this on April of the new Sgiesatthium— and faerself met, I made not heads nor heads of what sentence-like word-massings the Upling was siccing on me. Instead, dear Âhern to spare, Bryth sicced faer story on a feltsheet.

Lovelymost E, I give you Bryth’s letter. Never but —mine aside— to compliment your affections, enclosed are the original writeup and, by my pen, another reseasoned to your Anglophonic appetite. (Only the dearer for its abhorrence.)
